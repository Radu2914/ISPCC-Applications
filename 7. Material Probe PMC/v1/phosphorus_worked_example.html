Why negative? Iron's mass normalised by 56 gives x̃ = 0.9972 — almost exactly 1. At x = 1, sin(πx) ≈ 0 (the cascade completes). The cos(πx) term contributes −1/11, and sin(π²x) at this point is negative and carries weight 3/11. Iron sits at the inflection point of the π-basis — neither ascending nor descending. The negative Π score is physically correct: iron is the nuclear binding energy maximum, past which nucleosynthesis cascades in the other direction.
